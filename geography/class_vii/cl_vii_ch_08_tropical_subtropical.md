# Class VII : 8. Human Environment Interactions - The Tropical and the Subtropical Region

## UPSC Prelims Practice Questions

### Q1. Consider the following statements regarding the Amazon Basin:

1. Its location close to the Equator is associated with a hot and wet climate throughout the year.
2. Heavy rainfall supports the development of dense rainforest vegetation.
3. The dense canopy can restrict sunlight reaching the forest floor.
4. The high humidity of the region is primarily a consequence of the dense forest rather than its equatorial climatic setting.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1 and 4 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **A**

> **Explanation:** The Amazon Basin lies close to the Equator and experiences a hot and wet climate throughout the year. Heavy rainfall supports dense rainforest growth, while the dense canopy restricts sunlight from reaching the ground. The fourth statement reverses the relationship: the high humidity is a characteristic of the climatic conditions, not primarily a consequence of the forest.

### Q2. Consider the following statements regarding slash-and-burn agriculture:

1. Burning the cleared vegetation temporarily releases nutrients into the soil.
2. Continuous cultivation of the same patch can reduce soil fertility.
3. Abandonment of a depleted patch allows vegetation to regenerate and soil fertility to recover over time.
4. The practice necessarily involves permanent cultivation of the same plot.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** In slash-and-burn agriculture, vegetation is cut and burnt, temporarily releasing nutrients into the soil. Repeated cultivation eventually reduces soil fertility, after which the plot may be abandoned and allowed to regenerate. Thus, it is not based on permanent cultivation of the same plot.

### Q3. Consider the following statements:

1. Removal of forest cover in a region receiving heavy rainfall can accelerate loss of topsoil.
2. Dense vegetation can reduce the direct impact of rainfall on exposed soil.
3. Conversion of a rainforest into agricultural land necessarily improves the long-term soil fertility of the region.
4. Continued forest destruction can therefore have consequences extending beyond the immediate loss of vegetation.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 4 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** Vegetation provides protection to the soil. When forest cover is removed, heavy rainfall can wash away exposed topsoil. Therefore, forest destruction can lead to wider environmental consequences. The third statement is incorrect because conversion to agriculture does not necessarily improve long-term soil fertility.

### Q4. Consider the following statements regarding the distribution of population in the Ganga-Brahmaputra basin:

1. Flat and fertile plains generally provide more favourable conditions for dense human habitation than steep mountain terrain.
2. The availability of flat land can facilitate agricultural activities.
3. Paddy cultivation is favoured in areas receiving sufficient rainfall.
4. Population density is determined exclusively by the availability of agricultural land.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** The fertile and relatively flat plains provide favourable conditions for habitation and agriculture, while steep mountain terrain is less suitable for dense settlement. Paddy requires sufficient water and is therefore associated with areas receiving adequate rainfall. Population distribution, however, cannot be attributed exclusively to agricultural land.

### Q5. Assertion (A): Terrace farming can make cultivation possible on steep slopes.

### Reason (R): Terraces create relatively flat surfaces and reduce the rapid downslope flow of water.

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.<br>
<strong>B.</strong> Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.<br>
<strong>C.</strong> A is true, but R is false.<br>
<strong>D.</strong> A is false, but R is true.

<br>

**Answer**: **A**

> **Explanation:** Terraces are constructed on steep slopes to create flatter surfaces for cultivation. By reducing the effective slope, they also prevent water from running off rapidly.

### Q14. Consider the following statements regarding human settlement and river systems:

1. Large settlements tend to develop along major rivers partly because rivers provide access to water and support economic activities.
2. Urban growth along rivers can create environmental pressures when wastewater is discharged into them.
3. The presence of a river guarantees that the water body will remain suitable for all human and ecological uses.
4. River-based transport can contribute to the economic importance of settlements located along navigable stretches.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 4 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** Rivers have historically supported settlements and economic activities. However, concentration of towns and industries along rivers can also result in pollution through wastewater discharge. River transport can further enhance the economic significance of riverine settlements. The mere presence of a river does not guarantee good water quality.

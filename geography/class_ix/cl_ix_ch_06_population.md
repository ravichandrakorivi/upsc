### Q1. Consider the following statements regarding population density:

1. Population density is expressed as the number of persons per unit area.
2. Population density can reveal differences in the spatial concentration of population that total population figures alone may not show.
3. A country with a large population must necessarily have a higher population density than a country with a smaller population.
4. Population density is influenced by the size of the geographical area as well as the number of inhabitants.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1, 2 and 4 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **A**

> **Explanation:** Population density is the number of persons per unit area. It provides a better picture of uneven population distribution. Both population size and geographical area determine density; hence, a smaller-population country can have a higher density than a larger-population country.

### Q4. Consider the following statements:

1. Population growth refers to a change in the number of inhabitants over a specified period.
2. Absolute increase refers to the number of people added between two population counts.
3. Growth rate expresses the pace of population increase, generally as a percentage per annum.
4. Absolute increase and growth rate necessarily move in the same direction.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 3 and 4 only

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **A**

> **Explanation:** Absolute increase is the numerical addition to population, whereas growth rate measures the pace of increase. They need not move together because a lower growth rate applied to a much larger population can still produce a large absolute increase.

### Q6. Consider the following observations from India's population growth:

1. The annual growth rate increased between 1951 and 1981.
2. The growth rate subsequently began to decline.
3. Despite declining growth rates, absolute population additions remained very large.
4. A declining growth rate necessarily implies a declining total population.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 3 and 4 only

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** India's growth rate rose until around 1981 and declined thereafter. However, because the population base became very large, even a lower percentage growth produced substantial absolute additions. A declining growth rate does not mean that total population must decline.

3. Migration can alter both the size and distribution of population.
4. Internal migration changes the total population of the country.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>

### Q20. Consider the following statements regarding population as a dynamic phenomenon:

1. Population size can change through births, deaths and migration.
2. Population distribution can change even without a change in the country's total population.
3. Population composition can change as a result of migration.
4. A decline in the rate of natural increase necessarily causes an immediate decline in total population.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 3 and 4 only

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** Births, deaths and migration are the three main processes affecting population. Internal migration can redistribute population without changing national population size, and migration can alter age and sex composition. A declining rate of natural increase does not necessarily produce an immediate decline in total population.

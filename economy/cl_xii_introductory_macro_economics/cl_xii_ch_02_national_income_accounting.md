### Q1. Consider the following statements regarding final goods:

1. A good becomes a final good because of the economic nature of its use rather than merely because of its physical characteristics.
2. A good purchased by a household can be an intermediate good if it is subsequently used for further production.
3. The same commodity can be a final good in one transaction and an intermediate good in another.
4. All durable goods purchased by households are necessarily capital goods.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

<div>

<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 1, 2 and 3 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 2, 3 and 4 only<br>
<strong>D.</strong> 1, 2, 3 and 4

</div>

<br>

**Answer**: **B**

> **Explanation:** Whether a good is final or intermediate depends on its economic use. A good used for further production is intermediate, whereas a good purchased for final use is final. Consumer durables are final goods but are not capital goods merely because they are durable.

### Q4. Consider the following statements regarding stock and flow variables:
